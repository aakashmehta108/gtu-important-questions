# 3140915 Power Electronics: Important Questions

BE Semester 4, Electrical, GTU. Five questions per chapter, most repeated first.
Selected from GTU papers Summer 2026, Winter 2025, Summer 2025, matched with the official GTU syllabus.

## Unit 1: Power Switching Devices

1. Draw the static V-I characteristics of SCR and explain forward conduction, forward blocking and reverse blocking modes.
2. Draw the circuit symbols of power devices: MOSFET, IGBT, TRIAC, DIAC, GTO and UJT.
3. Explain the two-transistor model of SCR. List the SCR turn-on methods and explain R-C gate triggering (how firing angle is varied).
4. Explain the basic structure of IGBT with a diagram. Justify the use of IGBT in DC-DC and DC-AC converters.
5. Explain dv/dt protection of SCR and the need of a snubber circuit for reliable SCR operation.

## Unit 2: DC-DC Converters, Switching Voltage Regulators

1. Explain multi-quadrant operation of a DC-DC converter (two-quadrant and four-quadrant chopper operation with switching details).
2. Explain the working of a buck (step-down) converter with circuit diagram and waveforms, and derive its output voltage formula.
3. Explain the operation of a boost converter in continuous conduction mode with neat circuit diagram and waveforms.
4. What is an isolated converter? Draw and explain the working of a flyback converter.
5. Compare linear voltage regulator and switching voltage regulator.

## Unit 3: DC-AC Converters, Inverters

1. Explain the three-phase voltage source inverter with 120 degree mode of conduction, with circuit diagram and gate and phase voltage waveforms.
2. Explain the three-phase voltage source inverter with 180 degree mode of conduction, with circuit diagram and waveforms.
3. Explain the SPWM modulation technique for a single-phase full bridge inverter. Also describe bipolar and unipolar SPWM.
4. Describe the SVPWM (space vector PWM) control technique in brief.
5. Explain the working of a single-phase full bridge inverter with circuit diagram and waveforms, and discuss the effect of high switching frequency on its harmonic spectrum.

## Unit 4: AC-DC Converters

1. Explain the working of a single-phase full wave controlled rectifier with R-L load and freewheeling diode, with circuit diagram and waveforms. State the need of the freewheeling diode.
2. Derive the average output voltage equation of a single-phase full wave controlled rectifier with resistive load, for firing angle alpha.
3. Explain the working of a single-phase semi-converter with resistive load, with voltage and current waveforms.
4. Describe the three-phase full-wave controlled AC-DC converter with R load, with circuit diagram and waveforms.
5. Explain dual converter operation (circulating current mode) for obtaining variable positive and negative output voltages.

## Unit 5: AC Voltage Controller

1. Explain the single-phase full wave AC voltage controller with circuit diagram and necessary waveforms.
2. Give the difference between on-off control and phase angle control. Explain integral cycle control with equations and waveforms.
3. List industrial applications of AC voltage controllers and give their merits and demerits.
4. Apply an AC voltage controller for light dimmer control when the load is supplied from an AC source. Explain with the circuit.
5. Derive the expression for RMS output voltage of a single-phase AC voltage controller with resistive load.

## Unit 6: Miscellaneous

1. State the adverse effects of electromagnetic interference (EMI) due to power electronic converters and discuss remedial steps to reduce EMI.
2. Explain the working of a single-phase to single-phase cycloconverter with circuit diagram and waveforms, for output frequency 1/5th of the supply frequency (for example 10 Hz from 50 Hz).
3. Write a short note on matrix converter with its circuit diagram.
4. Discuss the midpoint cycloconverter in brief, and show that the fundamental RMS per-phase output voltage of an m-pulse cycloconverter is Vor = Vph (m/pi) sin(pi/m).
5. Enlist applications of power electronic switch based inverters in power systems and electrical drives.
