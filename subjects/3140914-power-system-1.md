# 3140914 Power System-I: Important Questions

BE Semester 4, Electrical, GTU. Five questions per chapter, most repeated first.
Selected from GTU papers Summer 2026, Winter 2025, Summer 2025, Winter 2024, matched with the official GTU syllabus.

## Unit 1: Conventional Generation, Load Curves and Tariffs

1. Define tariff. Explain the types of tariff in detail, with the two-part tariff.
2. Define load curve, load factor, demand factor and diversity factor. Draw a typical daily load curve.
3. Draw the schematic layout of a steam power station and explain the function of superheater, economiser and ID-FD fans.
4. Compare steam power station and hydro power station (efficiency, initial cost, running cost, site).
5. Draw the schematic arrangement of a nuclear power station and explain the function of each block.

## Unit 2: Introduction to Wind and Solar Power Generation

1. Explain the equivalent circuit of a solar cell and draw its V-I and P-V characteristics.
2. Compare stand-alone PV system and grid-connected PV system with diagrams (any three to five points).
3. Explain the various components of a wind energy conversion system with a diagram.
4. Differentiate constant speed SCIG and WRIG types of wind generation units.
5. Compare horizontal axis wind turbine (HAWT) and vertical axis wind turbine (VAWT).

## Unit 3: Electrical Supply Systems

1. Explain the types of distribution systems with diagrams (radial, ring main and interconnected).
2. Classify AC supply systems by number of phases and wires. Give the advantages of high voltage transmission.
3. Numerical: an AC distributor AB has concentrated loads at points C and B. Calculate the voltage at each load point and the voltage drops, with a phasor diagram (load power factors referred to their load voltages).
4. Numerical: non-reactive loads are connected between neutral and the red, yellow and blue phases of a 3-phase 4-wire supply. Find the current in each line and in the neutral wire.
5. Draw the configuration of a bundled conductor and explain its effect on the transmission line.

## Unit 4: Power Factor and Power Factor Improvement of Load

1. What is power factor? State the causes of low power factor and its disadvantages.
2. Explain the different methods of power factor improvement.
3. Explain power factor improvement by a synchronous condenser with a diagram, and give its advantages and disadvantages.
4. Derive an expression for the most economical power factor that can be attained by a consumer.
5. Numerical: an alternator supplies a load at 0.8 lagging pf. If the pf is raised to unity, how much more kW can it supply for the same kVA loading?

## Unit 5: Mechanical Features and Design of Overhead Transmission Line

1. Define string efficiency. Derive the expression for string efficiency of a 3-disc suspension insulator string.
2. Numerical: a 3-disc string has shunt capacitance equal to a given fraction (for example 1/9) of the disc capacitance. Find the voltage across each disc and the string efficiency.
3. State and explain the methods of improving string efficiency (capacitance grading, guard ring, longer cross arm). Discuss any one in detail.
4. Derive the expression for sag of a conductor when the supports are at equal level.
5. Numerical on sag: given span, conductor weight per metre, tension or ultimate strength and factor of safety, calculate the maximum sag.

## Unit 6: Transmission Line Parameters

1. Derive the inductance of a 3-phase transmission line with symmetrical spacing, L = 2 x 10^-7 ln(D/r') H/m per phase.
2. Derive the capacitance to neutral of a 3-phase transmission line with equilateral (symmetrical) spacing.
3. Explain self GMD and mutual GMD. Numerical: find the inductance per km of a double circuit 3-phase line by the GMD method.
4. What is transposition of conductors? Why is it necessary and what is its effect on line performance?
5. Explain skin effect, proximity effect and Ferranti effect. Why is skin effect absent in a DC system?

## Unit 7: Underground Cables

1. What is grading of cables? Explain capacitance grading and intersheath grading, and compare the two.
2. Derive the expression for maximum dielectric stress in a single-core cable and show where it occurs.
3. Draw the construction of a high voltage single core cable and state the function of the metallic sheath, bedding, armouring and serving.
4. Derive the expression for insulation resistance of a single-core cable.
5. Numerical: find the capacitance of a single-core cable of given conductor radius, sheath radius, length and relative permittivity of insulation.

## Unit 8: Substations

1. Define substation. Explain the classification of substations in different ways.
2. Draw the single line diagram of a 66/11 kV substation and label each equipment.
3. Draw the layout and connection diagram of a pole-mounted distribution substation and explain how it functions.
4. Compare outdoor substation and indoor substation.
5. Sketch the duplicate busbar system and explain its operation under fault condition.

## Unit 9: Neutral Grounding

1. What is neutral grounding? List the different methods of neutral grounding and give the advantages of neutral grounding.
2. Explain solid grounding and resistance grounding, with advantages and disadvantages of each.
3. Explain resonant grounding. Numerical: calculate the inductance and inductive reactance of a Peterson coil for a given 3-phase line capacitance to ground.
4. State the advantages of a grounded neutral system and the disadvantages of an ungrounded neutral system when a single line to ground fault occurs.
5. Describe the ungrounded or isolated neutral system and its disadvantages.
