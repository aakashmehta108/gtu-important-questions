# BE03009031 Electrical Circuit Analysis: Important Questions

BE Semester 3, Electrical, GTU. Five questions per chapter, most repeated first.
Selected from GTU papers Summer 2026, Winter 2025, Summer 2025, Winter 2024 of old-syllabus equivalent subject 3130906, matched with the official GTU syllabus.

## Unit 1: Network Theorems and Coupled Circuits

1. State and explain the Superposition theorem. Give the step-by-step method of solving a network with it. Numerical: find the voltage across a resistor in a two-source network using superposition.
2. State the Maximum Power Transfer theorem for AC circuits and write its conditions (load variable in resistance only, in reactance only, and in both). Numerical: find the value of RL for maximum power and the value of Pmax.
3. State Thevenin's and Norton's theorems. Give the step-by-step solution using Thevenin's theorem. Numerical: determine the load current in a network using Thevenin's theorem.
4. Numerical practice set: find node voltages in a network using node analysis, and find the current through a resistor using mesh analysis.
5. Explain the dot convention in coupled circuits with an example. Numerical: two mutually coupled inductors (L1, L2, M given) are connected in series, find the equivalent inductance for series aiding and series opposing connections.

## Unit 2: Initial and Final Conditions

1. Explain the initial conditions in resistor, inductor and capacitor. What is the importance of initial conditions in network analysis?
2. Give reasons: the current through an inductor cannot change instantaneously, and the voltage across a capacitor cannot change instantaneously.
3. Numerical: a switching circuit reaches steady state, then the switch is operated at t = 0. Determine the values of i, di/dt and d²i/dt² (or of voltage) at t = 0- and t = 0+.
4. Define and explain the time constant of a series R-L circuit and a series R-C circuit.
5. Explain the transient response of a series R-C circuit with DC excitation. Also find the particular solution for current i(t) of a series R-L-C circuit energised by a DC source, capacitor initially uncharged.

## Unit 3: Sinusoidal Steady State Analysis

1. Define the terms RMS value, active power, apparent power, complex power and power factor for AC circuits.
2. Numerical: an unknown impedance Z is connected in a single-phase AC circuit, current/voltage phasors are given, find the value of Z.
3. Numerical: a delta-connected 3-phase load with given phase impedances is supplied from a balanced 3-phase supply, determine the phase currents and line currents.
4. Discuss resonance in a series R-L-C circuit: condition of resonance, resonant frequency, quality factor and bandwidth.
5. Explain the phasor representation of a sinusoidal quantity. Define impedance and admittance and draw phasor diagrams for series R-L and R-C circuits.

## Unit 4: Electrical Circuit Analysis Using Laplace Transforms

1. Define poles and zeros of a network function and explain their significance. Numerical: for a given LC network, determine the transform impedance Z(s) and plot its poles and zeros.
2. Numerical: using the Laplace transform method, find i(t) or v(t) for a switching RL/RC/RLC circuit with given initial conditions (switch operated at t = 0 after steady state).
3. Derive the step response of a series R-C circuit using the Laplace transform. (Same method for series R-L circuit.)
4. State and explain the properties of the Laplace transform, and the advantages of the Laplace transform method over the classical method.
5. Prove the Initial Value theorem and the Final Value theorem of the Laplace transform.

## Unit 5: Two Port Network and Network Functions

1. Derive the relationships between two-port parameters: h-parameters in terms of z-parameters, Y-parameters in terms of Z-parameters, and ABCD-parameters in terms of z or y parameters.
2. Write the symmetry and reciprocity conditions for z, y, ABCD and h parameters of a two-port network.
3. Define Z-parameters (open-circuit impedance parameters) and draw their equivalent circuit. Numerical: obtain the Z-parameters for a given two-port network.
4. Define Y-parameters (short-circuit admittance parameters) and h-parameters, and draw the equivalent circuit of a two-port network in terms of Y-parameters and in terms of h-parameters.
5. Explain the interconnection of two-port networks: series-series, series-parallel and parallel-parallel connections, and which parameters add in each connection.
